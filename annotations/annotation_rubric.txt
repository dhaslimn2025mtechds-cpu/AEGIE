AEGIE HUMAN ANNOTATION RUBRIC
=============================

Rubric Version:
V2

Purpose:
This rubric is used by human annotators to evaluate genuine
participant responses in the AEGIE research study.

Each response is scored independently from 1 to 5 on five
dimensions:

1. Relevance
2. Technical Correctness
3. Clarity
4. Communication Quality
5. Overall Answer Quality


============================================================
GENERAL ANNOTATION RULES
============================================================

1. Use whole-number scores only:

   1, 2, 3, 4, or 5

2. Score each dimension independently.

3. Do not automatically calculate one score from another score.

4. Overall Answer Quality is a separate holistic judgment.
   It should not simply be an arithmetic average of the other
   four scores.

5. Annotators must evaluate the participant's actual response,
   not the AEGIE prediction.

6. Annotators must not see AEGIE predicted scores while
   creating human reference labels.

7. If the automated transcript appears incorrect, the
   annotator should use the original audio recording where
   available before assigning a score.

8. A participant must not be penalized for an automatic
   speech-recognition transcription error.

9. Accent or regional pronunciation must not reduce a score
   unless it genuinely prevents the answer from being
   understood.

10. Do not reward an answer simply because it uses the same
    wording as a reference answer.

11. Technical correctness must be judged based on the meaning
    of the participant's answer.

12. Different technically valid explanations may receive high
    scores.

13. Missing, corrupted, silent, or otherwise unusable
    responses must not receive invented scores.

14. If a response cannot be reliably evaluated, mark it
    according to the annotation protocol instead of assigning
    an artificial score.

15. Annotators should make their first judgment independently
    before any disagreement discussion or adjudication.


============================================================
1. RELEVANCE
============================================================

Definition:

Relevance measures how directly the response addresses the
question that was asked.

Score 1:
Completely unrelated to the question.

The response does not meaningfully address the requested topic.

Score 2:
Mostly unrelated.

The response contains very limited information related to the
question but does not meaningfully answer it.

Score 3:
Partially relevant.

The response addresses part of the question but misses one or
more important aspects.

Score 4:
Relevant.

The response directly addresses the question and covers most
important points, with only minor omissions.

Score 5:
Highly relevant and complete.

The response directly and completely addresses the question
without unnecessary diversion.


============================================================
2. TECHNICAL CORRECTNESS
============================================================

Definition:

Technical Correctness measures whether the technical content
of the response is factually and conceptually correct for the
question and job-role context.

Score 1:
Fundamentally incorrect.

The main technical explanation is wrong or demonstrates a
serious misunderstanding of the concept.

Score 2:
Major technical errors.

Some relevant ideas may be present, but important technical
claims are incorrect.

Score 3:
Partially correct.

The core idea is partly correct but contains missing details,
minor misconceptions, or incomplete reasoning.

Score 4:
Mostly correct.

The main technical explanation is correct with only minor
omissions, simplifications, or small inaccuracies.

Score 5:
Technically correct and well explained.

The response demonstrates accurate understanding and explains
the important technical concept clearly and appropriately.

Important:

A response does not need to use the exact wording of a
reference answer to receive a high score.

Any technically valid explanation should be evaluated fairly.


============================================================
3. CLARITY
============================================================

Definition:

Clarity measures how understandable, logically organized, and
easy to follow the content of the answer is.

This dimension mainly evaluates the organization and
understandability of the ideas.

Score 1:
Very difficult to understand.

The response is highly confusing, disorganized, or impossible
to follow.

Score 2:
Frequently unclear.

The main idea is difficult to follow because of poor structure
or unclear explanation.

Score 3:
Understandable but lacks structure.

The main meaning can be understood, but the response may be
somewhat disorganized or incomplete.

Score 4:
Clear and reasonably structured.

The response is easy to understand and follows a mostly logical
order.

Score 5:
Very clear, concise, and logically structured.

The ideas are communicated in a well-organized way with very
little unnecessary or confusing content.


============================================================
4. COMMUNICATION QUALITY
============================================================

Definition:

Communication Quality measures how effectively the participant
communicates the answer verbally.

This may include understandable delivery, sentence formation,
appropriate pace, and the ability to communicate the intended
meaning.

Do not penalize a participant merely for accent or regional
pronunciation.

Score 1:
Communication seriously prevents understanding.

The listener cannot reliably understand the intended response.

Score 2:
Frequent communication difficulties.

Delivery problems repeatedly interfere with understanding.

Score 3:
Adequate communication.

The response can be understood, although some delivery or
language difficulties are present.

Score 4:
Good communication.

The response is communicated effectively with only minor
delivery difficulties.

Score 5:
Highly effective and professional communication.

The participant communicates the answer confidently and
effectively, and the intended meaning is consistently easy to
understand.


============================================================
5. OVERALL ANSWER QUALITY
============================================================

Definition:

Overall Answer Quality is the annotator's holistic evaluation
of the complete response.

It considers the response as a whole, including its relevance,
technical value, clarity, and communication effectiveness.

The overall score must be judged independently and must not be
automatically calculated as the average of the other scores.

Score 1:
Very poor response.

The answer fails to meaningfully address the question or has
major problems that prevent it from being useful.

Score 2:
Weak response.

The answer demonstrates limited understanding or substantial
problems in content or communication.

Score 3:
Acceptable response.

The answer demonstrates basic understanding and communicates a
reasonable response but has noticeable limitations.

Score 4:
Good response.

The answer is relevant, mostly correct, clear, and effective,
with only minor limitations.

Score 5:
Excellent response.

The answer is highly relevant, technically strong, clear,
well organized, and effectively communicated.


============================================================
UNUSABLE RESPONSE RULE
============================================================

Do not assign artificial 1-5 scores when a response cannot be
evaluated reliably because of conditions such as:

- missing audio
- empty recording
- corrupted recording
- no understandable speech
- recording failure
- participant did not answer
- serious technical failure that prevents evaluation

Such cases should be documented using the annotation status or
annotation notes according to the AEGIE annotation protocol.


============================================================
ANNOTATOR INDEPENDENCE
============================================================

Each pilot response should be independently evaluated by at
least two human annotators.

Example annotator IDs:

RATER_01
RATER_02

The annotators should complete their initial scoring
independently.

Differences between annotators will later be analyzed using
inter-rater agreement methods.

Human annotation scores are research reference labels.

They must never be generated or filled automatically by AEGIE.
